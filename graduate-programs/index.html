What makes a graduate programme different

Strong programmes combine real responsibility with rotations, formal training, mentoring and progression criteria. Check whether sponsorship is available before investing in a long application.

  • Graduation-year eligibility
  • Work-authorisation and sponsorship
  • Rotations and permanent-role pathway
  • Assessment stages
  • Salary, benefits and location

Prepare for multi-stage assessment

Graduate recruitment may include online tests, recorded questions, an assessment centre and final interviews. Learn what each stage measures, practise under realistic time limits and request an accommodation through the official process when needed.

Use examples from coursework, projects, employment and volunteering. Recruiters usually need evidence of judgement, collaboration and delivery—not an inflated job title.

Check visa sponsorship before applying

Terms such as global programme or international company do not guarantee sponsorship. Look for the exact work location and right-to-work statement, then ask recruitment for written clarification if the vacancy is ambiguous.

Prepare for multi-stage selection

Many programmes use an application form, online tests, recorded interview, assessment centre and final interview. Practise the published format and keep examples showing analysis, teamwork, ownership and learning.

Apply early when recruitment is rolling, but never rush past a mandatory eligibility condition or submit unverified claims.

Frequently asked questions

Is a graduate programme a master's degree?+

No. In this context it is an employer's structured job or rotation programme for recent graduates.

Do global graduate programmes sponsor visas?+

Not automatically. Sponsorship depends on the employer, role, location and current immigration policy. Read the exact vacancy wording.
