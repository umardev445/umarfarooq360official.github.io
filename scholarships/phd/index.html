Scholarship route or funded position?

Doctoral funding can follow two models. In scholarship-led systems you apply for an award and a university place; in project-led systems you apply to a funded research vacancy much like a job. Some routes require supervisor support before formal submission.

  • Read the department's admission sequence
  • Map your methods to the proposed research
  • Contact supervisors only with a specific fit
  • Prepare a focused proposal when required
  • Check funding duration and teaching duties

Evaluate the funding package before accepting

A funded PhD offer should state the stipend, tuition coverage, duration, conditions and any teaching or research duties. Compare the net support with local housing, health insurance and visa costs, and ask how later years are renewed.

Confirm who controls the project direction, what happens if a supervisor leaves and whether conference, fieldwork or research costs have separate budgets. These questions affect the quality of the doctorate as much as the headline funding amount.

Compare the doctorate and the funding agreement

Use the listing cards to find possibilities, then compare the model that actually fits your qualification, goal and financing needs. Routes with similar funding labels can require different application owners and documents.

Keep a small, evidence-based shortlist

For each option, save the official source, cycle, nationality or residence conditions, programme, required documents and deadline. Mark a condition as unconfirmed when the current source is silent. A pending call is not an open application.

Prepare documents around the earliest dependent task. References, school nomination, programme admission and a scholarship form may have separate dates. Keep academic fit and net cost visible so a well-written application does not lead to an unaffordable offer.
