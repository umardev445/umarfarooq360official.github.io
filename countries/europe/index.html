Build a country-by-country shortlist

Erasmus Mundus degrees span multiple institutions and countries, while DAAD and Stipendium Hungaricum operate within national systems. Tuition, visa rules and work options still depend on the host country.

Compare the programme consortium, mobility path, language, degree award and funding terms—not just the headline destination.

  • Check whether the degree moves between countries
  • Confirm scholarship and programme deadlines
  • Review residence-permit rules for each mobility stage
  • Compare living allowances with local costs
  • Verify the final degree title and awarding institutions

Three funding routes that work differently

Erasmus Mundus funding is tied to a specific joint master's programme, whose consortium sets language, admission and scholarship instructions. DAAD EPOS supports eligible development-related postgraduate courses in Germany, with course-specific applications and professional-experience conditions. Stipendium Hungaricum depends on a country sending partner, eligible Hungarian university courses and a nomination stage.

Do not apply a rule from one route to another. Check the official course and programme pages before deciding whether a degree, applicant nationality or language document qualifies.

  • Erasmus: programme consortium and mobility plan
  • Germany: course application and DAAD eligibility
  • Hungary: sending partner plus host admission

Budget for the exact location and mobility pattern

For a joint degree, note where each semester is taught, what travel is provided and whether visa or residence permissions change along the way. For a single-country award, compare the stipend and housing support with that city's actual expenses.

Build a shortlist with degree title, official source, eligibility, confirmed deadline, funding exclusions and your next step. Consider the UK scholarship routes separately when comparing Europe because their university-offer and citizenship rules differ.

Italy: connect funding to the regional and university system

Italy adds three routes to a European shortlist: the national MAECI call, regional DSU benefits and university merit or fee-waiver schemes. Applicants must identify the correct regional body and should not treat one DSU deadline or economic threshold as a national rule.

Use the Italy hub to compare selected university fees, language evidence, admission and Universitaly pre-enrolment alongside funding. This prevents a scholarship shortlist from ignoring the separate course and visa sequence.

Frequently asked questions

Is there one scholarship application for studying in Europe?+

No. Erasmus Mundus, DAAD, Stipendium Hungaricum and university awards use different application systems. Start with the exact programme and country, then follow that programme owner's current instructions.

Does a European scholarship automatically cover every living cost?+

Not necessarily. Tuition, stipend, accommodation, insurance and travel coverage differ by award and city. Compare the official benefit list with the cost of the actual study location.

Should I choose a country or a scholarship first?+

Choose the degree and realistic destinations together. A strong shortlist matches academic fit, eligibility, funding and the admission route rather than chasing a funding label alone.
