Scholarships without IELTS for international students in 2027

A scholarship can accept an application without IELTS while a university still requires language evidence. Use this guide to distinguish a real alternative from an unverified waiver claim.

Updated 13 Oct 2026

The short answer

Without IELTS does not necessarily mean without English evidence. A university may accept a different test or a narrowly defined exemption; the scholarship or visa process can have separate conditions.

A practical English-waiver check

Record four separate decisions: the programme's required level, accepted certificates, whether your previous qualification meets an exemption, and the evidence deadline. Do not treat a country-wide list as an admission guarantee.

Padua and Polimi do not use identical exemption rules. An English-taught degree from Pakistan is not automatically a Padua exemption. Use the institution-specific guidance linked here before deciding that IELTS is unnecessary.

  • Save the official requirement for your intake.
  • Ask about your qualification, not a generic no-IELTS route.
  • Keep a recognised-test fallback if the exemption is unclear.

Check three separate rulebooks

Before adding an opportunity to your shortlist, check the scholarship owner, the university or consortium delivering the degree and the immigration authority for the study destination. The strictest applicable rule determines what you will ultimately need.

  • Scholarship rule: what must be submitted for funding consideration?
  • Admission rule: what does the exact university and programme accept?
  • Visa rule: what English evidence can the immigration process require?
  • Timing rule: must the evidence be ready at application, offer or visa stage?

Five common alternatives to IELTS

An IELTS waiver does not create one universal replacement. Universities choose their own accepted evidence and can change it between programmes or intakes. Confirm the required score, test version, validity period and whether online or at-home tests are accepted.

  • Alternative test: TOEFL, PTE Academic, Cambridge English or Duolingo where explicitly accepted
  • Medium of Instruction: an official letter confirming that a previous qualification was taught and assessed in English
  • Previous English-medium qualification: transcripts or certificates from an accepted institution or country
  • Internal assessment: a university interview, written test or language placement process
  • Language pathway: a conditional offer or funded preparatory year before the main degree

Scholarship routes to research for 2027

The programmes below illustrate different meanings of 'without IELTS.' They are not a promise that every applicant or course receives a waiver. Open the current official call and the chosen university's programme page before applying.

  • Chevening Scholarships: Chevening removed its own English-language test requirement in 2020, but applicants must still meet the requirements of their chosen UK universities and secure an unconditional offer by the relevant deadline.
  • Türkiye Scholarships: language-test results are requested when the selected university or programme requires them. Applicants choosing an English-medium programme may still need an internationally accepted certificate, while many awardees also complete Turkish-language preparation.
  • MEXT Scholarship, Japan: IELTS is not presented as one universal Embassy Recommendation application document. Applicants must follow the current country-specific embassy process, official guidelines and required language examinations or interviews for their category.
  • Stipendium Hungaricum: the host institution sets the required language level. Some programmes accept proof that previous study was completed in the programme language, while others specify IELTS, TOEFL or another certificate.
  • Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters: each consortium publishes its own admission and language requirements. Full scholarships may be available, but an IELTS waiver for one course cannot be assumed for another course.

What an MOI certificate should contain

A Medium of Instruction certificate is normally issued by the registrar, controller of examinations or another authorised office at your previous institution. It confirms the language used to teach and assess your completed qualification. A casual letter from a lecturer may not be sufficient.

Ask the target university whether it accepts MOI for your nationality, previous institution, qualification level and chosen programme. Some universities accept it only when the entire qualification was taught and examined in English, and some may request an interview or additional evidence.

  • Your full name and degree title
  • Institution name and study dates
  • A clear statement that teaching and assessment were conducted in English
  • Official letterhead, issue date, signature and institutional stamp where used
  • Contact details that allow the university to verify the document

Important guidance for Pakistani students

Many Pakistani applicants can obtain an English-proficiency or Medium of Instruction letter from their university, but acceptance is not automatic. The receiving institution decides whether the document meets its policy. Ask for confirmation that names your exact programme and intake rather than relying on a general social-media answer.

For scholarship routes involving HEC Pakistan, check both the HEC call and the international scholarship portal. For example, current HEC guidance for Stipendium Hungaricum notes that some Hungarian universities require IELTS or TOEFL, while many institutions accept an English-proficiency certificate. HEC nomination tests and document rules remain separate from the host university's language requirement.

  • Request the MOI or English-proficiency certificate early from the authorised university office
  • Use the same spelling of your name shown on your passport and academic records
  • Check whether HEC nomination, attestation or testing is also required
  • Keep written confirmation from the host university
  • Do not pay an agent for a guaranteed waiver, admission or scholarship

Scholarship requirement and university requirement are different

Government and external scholarships often fund study at several universities. The scholarship owner can remove its own test condition without controlling every university's admission policy. Chevening is a useful example: its own English requirement was removed, but the applicant must still satisfy the selected university and obtain an unconditional offer.

The reverse can also occur. A university may accept MOI for admission while an external scholarship, professional regulator or specific course requires a standardised test. Verify the exact course rather than only the university homepage.

Do not forget the student visa stage

Admission and visa decisions are separate. A university waiver confirms what the institution accepted; it does not automatically prove that every immigration route will accept the same evidence. Visa rules can depend on the country, institution, course level and how the university recorded the language assessment.

Use the destination government's current immigration website and the university's international office for guidance. Avoid relying on an old screenshot or a consultant's general statement, because visa and evidence rules can change between intakes.

A safe application process without IELTS

Treat language evidence as part of eligibility research, not as a question to solve after selection. Complete the steps below for every programme in your shortlist and save the evidence with your application records.

  • 1. Open the current scholarship call on the programme owner's official domain
  • 2. Find the exact degree on the university or consortium website
  • 3. Read the English-language section for your applicant category
  • 4. Confirm whether MOI or another test is accepted and at what stage
  • 5. Check student-visa evidence on the destination government's website
  • 6. Email admissions if the published policy does not answer your case
  • 7. Save the reply, policy URL and date checked
  • 8. Keep a test-based backup for programmes that do not confirm a waiver

Warning signs in no-IELTS scholarship lists

A page can use a real scholarship name and still give unsafe language advice. Verify every claim when a list does not distinguish the scholarship from university admission, fails to name an intake or uses phrases such as 'guaranteed without IELTS.'

  • No link or reference to the current official call
  • One rule is claimed for every university in a country
  • The page confuses no IELTS with no language evidence
  • Closed deadlines are presented as currently open
  • A programme-specific waiver is described as a scholarship-wide rule
  • An agent asks for payment to guarantee a waiver or award

When taking IELTS may still be the better option

A waiver can reduce cost or help when a deadline is close, but a recognised test score can expand the number of universities available to you. It may also make your evidence easier to compare across several countries and reduce uncertainty where programme or visa rules are strict.

Base the decision on your complete shortlist. If most of your preferred programmes require a standardised test, preparing once may be more efficient than building a narrow shortlist around exceptions. If several strong-fit programmes confirm MOI or another alternative in writing, a waiver route may be reasonable.

Compare the route, not the 'IELTS-free' headline

Chevening removed its own English test condition, yet each selected UK university still sets admission requirements. The Japanese MEXT route depends on scholarship category and embassy or university instructions. A Stipendium Hungaricum applicant must satisfy the selected host institution's course language rule. These are three different checks, not three blanket exemptions.

For every course, save the institution's actual accepted evidence and the rule's date. A Medium of Instruction letter is a request for an institution-specific decision, not a guarantee of acceptance.

  • Programme: does the award itself demand an IELTS result?
  • Course: what evidence does the exact university accept?
  • Immigration: what proof does the destination require?
  • Timing: at which stage must each item be ready?

An email that resolves a real language-rule uncertainty

Send admissions your intended degree and intake, citizenship, prior university and degree, and the wording your institution can provide on its official Medium of Instruction letter. Ask whether this document satisfies their published English condition for that particular programme. Keep the written answer with your application file.

If the answer is no, compare other officially accepted tests rather than relying on a third-party list. Policies can differ even between two degrees at the same university.

Italy: check the programme, certificate and MOI route

Italy has no single IELTS rule covering every university. The University of Padua tells applicants to follow the B1 or B2 level in the individual degree call and publishes accepted certificates; it also describes a route for a completed qualification taught entirely in English when official records prove the teaching language.

Politecnico di Milano currently lists IELTS 6, TOEFL iBT 78 and PTE Academic 59 among accepted master's evidence. These examples belong to named institutions and current rules. Use the dedicated Italy admission guide to verify the exact degree and intake before relying on an MOI letter or test score.

Compare waiver claims with the real admission rule

Treat every 'without IELTS' claim as a question for the exact university and degree. Record the accepted test, section minimum, medium-of-instruction rule or written waiver before deciding whether to skip a test.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a fully funded scholarship without IELTS?+

Possibly. Some scholarship routes do not impose a universal IELTS condition, but the selected university may still require IELTS, another test, MOI or an internal assessment. Verify the scholarship, admission and visa rules separately.

Is an MOI certificate accepted instead of IELTS?+

Some institutions accept an official Medium of Instruction certificate, while others do not. Acceptance can depend on your previous qualification, institution, nationality, programme and intake. Obtain written confirmation from the admitting university.

Can Pakistani students apply for scholarships without IELTS?+

Yes, when the scholarship and selected institution accept an alternative such as MOI, another test or an interview. Pakistani applicants must also check any HEC nomination, testing and document requirements that apply to the scholarship route.

Does an IELTS waiver also cover the student visa?+

Not automatically. University admission and immigration are separate processes. Check the destination government's current student-visa rules and ask the university how your English ability will be recorded for visa purposes.

Which English tests may be accepted instead of IELTS?+

Depending on the programme, alternatives can include TOEFL, PTE Academic, Cambridge English or the Duolingo English Test. Accepted versions, scores and validity periods vary, so use the exact programme page rather than a general list.

Should I take IELTS even if a waiver is available?+

Consider your whole shortlist. A test score may open more universities and reduce uncertainty, while a confirmed waiver may save time and cost. Choose the route that keeps the strongest-fit programmes available to you.

Does 'without IELTS' mean no English proof is required?+

No. A scholarship may have no IELTS condition while the university still requires another test, a Medium of Instruction letter or other accepted evidence.

Is a Medium of Instruction letter accepted by every university?+

No. Acceptance is institution- and programme-specific. Ask the admissions office for the exact intake and save the written response before relying on an MOI letter.

Should I check visa language rules separately?+

Yes. Scholarship, university admission and immigration requirements are separate checks. Meeting one does not automatically satisfy the others.
