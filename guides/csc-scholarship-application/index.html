How to apply for a CSC scholarship: a submission checklist

Step 1: capture the exact application instructions

Save the current call as a dated note with its URL. Record the degree, subject, teaching language, receiving organisation and deadline. If the notice links to an older detail page, do not silently combine dates or fees from different years. Ask the programme owner which instructions control the current cycle.

Use your own email address for accounts and retain access to it throughout review. Keep passwords private. An adviser can help organise an application without controlling your credentials. Start from the official university or authority website rather than from a payment request sent through social media.

Step 2: build a document register

Separate documents you already possess from those requiring another person or organisation. Transcripts, degree certificates, translations, recommendations and test results can take different amounts of time. Check whether the current call requests originals, notarised scans or certified translations before buying a service.

Step 3: write a programme-specific study plan

Describe the question or skill gap you want to address, your relevant preparation and why the selected programme is suitable. For a research degree, explain a feasible research question, proposed approach and supervision fit. For a taught degree, connect the curriculum to a concrete academic or professional goal.

Use evidence rather than inflated claims. One well-explained project can be stronger than a list of unrelated achievements. State your own contribution, methods, result and what you learned. Respect the university’s language and word-count rules; this guide does not impose a national word count.

Worked example

Planning example: connect an undergraduate project on irrigation data to a proposed master’s research question about sensor reliability. Explain the data you could access and the methods you need to learn. Do not claim results you have not produced.

Step 4: manage supervisors and references professionally

Where the programme requires supervisor agreement or pre-admission, begin that process before the portal deadline. Send a concise introduction, academic CV and focused research outline. A reply expressing interest is not necessarily the formal acceptance document required by the call.

Give recommenders the programme name, submission method and realistic deadline. Supply a factual summary of your work together so the letter can be specific. Never write a fictitious endorsement or upload someone’s signature without authorisation.

Step 5: review both systems before submitting

Copying data across portals creates opportunities for contradictions. Compare degree title, start date, passport name and study language in every form. Review the generated application PDF as well as the input screen. Some portals restrict editing after submission; do not use the final submit action as a test.

Step 6: track requests after submission

Create a simple log of submission time, application number, portal status and any request for corrections or interviews. Check the portal as well as email and spam folders. Respond through the official channel and keep a copy of important replies. A status such as reviewed, nominated or pre-admitted should not be rewritten as awarded.

If a portal fails near the deadline, save screenshots showing the time and error and contact the responsible office promptly. There is no guarantee of an extension. After an offer, compare funding terms and registration requirements before making travel commitments.

Frequently asked questions

Do I submit only in the CSC system?+

Not necessarily. Many routes also require a university or national nomination application. Follow the current call.

How long should the study plan be?+

Use the programme’s own word-count and language requirements. There is no single length appropriate for every host.

Can I edit after submission?+

Portal rules differ. Review everything first and ask the responsible office about corrections rather than assuming editing remains available.
