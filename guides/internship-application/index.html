How to apply for international internships

Turn a real vacancy into a focused application. Check eligibility and practical conditions first, then demonstrate the required skills through projects, coursework or experience you can explain.

Updated 24 Oct 2026Official source record ↓

The short answer

Start with the role’s eligibility, location, duties, payment and work-authorisation conditions. Then tailor your CV and cover letter to the actual tasks and submit through the employer’s stated channel.

Next step: Use the before-and-after CV bullet and cover-letter example below, then check the placement’s costs, supervision and application receipt.

Decide whether the placement is usable before applying

An impressive organisation name does not establish that the role fits your degree, budget or right to work. The employer’s current vacancy is the source for eligibility and benefits; an internship listing is not a visa decision.

Rewrite a CV bullet around work and results

A useful bullet states the task, your action and the output. Add a figure when it is measured and helps explain scale. A classroom exercise can demonstrate preparation without being described as employment.

Keep the project title and context accurate. Explain your part of a team effort, distinguish a prototype from a deployed product and avoid claims about business impact you did not measure.

Teaching example

Before and after — fictional project

Before: Worked on a university data project and learned teamwork.

After: Built a validation sheet for a course dataset, documented inconsistent entries and summarised the limits of the final comparison in the team report.

The revised bullet shows work and evidence without inventing revenue, employment or a measured percentage.

Write a short cover letter with one clear example

Open with the exact role and your strongest reason for fit. Use one or two examples to prove relevant preparation. Explain why the team or work interests you using details from the real vacancy. End with the required availability or next step rather than repeating your entire CV.

The fictional example below demonstrates the sequence. A real application needs the correct employer, role, dates and evidence.

Teaching example

Fictional cover-letter example

Dear Recruitment Team, I am applying for the research internship because its focus on organising and communicating evidence matches the work I have practised in my final-year project.

In that project, I created validation rules for our observations, recorded errors and revised the collection sheet after feedback. I also wrote a short explanation of the findings and their limitations. This experience would help me contribute to careful data preparation while learning your team's methods.

I am interested in the specific responsibilities published in your vacancy and would confirm my availability and location requirements in the application form. Thank you for considering my application.

Not an application to a real vacancy. Adapt with your own evidence and the employer's instructions.

Verify the role before investing in the application

Open the vacancy on the employer's official careers domain. Record its reference, team, location, dates, responsibilities, essential requirements and application route. A copied listing can help you discover a role, but it is not the final instruction.

Check student or recent-graduate status, permitted countries, working hours, pay and immigration responsibility. Remote describes an arrangement, not worldwide eligibility. If a condition is unclear, ask recruitment a precise question before presenting yourself as eligible.

Separate essential and preferred requirements

Read the vacancy as a selection checklist. Mandatory conditions decide whether the role is possible. Preferred skills tell you where stronger evidence may help. Do not spend the entire cover letter proving an optional skill while ignoring an essential requirement.

If experience can come from coursework or volunteering, select the most relevant example. Do not claim a tool because you used a different tool with a similar name. You can explain a transferable method and accurately describe what you have learned.

Check the placement's practical value

Compare the work, supervision, learning objectives, hours and financial conditions. A recognisable organisation can still have a role that does not match your learning need or permitted work location. A stipend may not cover the full cost of a placement abroad.

Ask what you would produce, who would review it and how feedback is organised. For remote work, clarify equipment, internet, security and time-zone overlap. For an on-site role, confirm travel and immigration support before accepting.

Prepare for tests and interviews

Selection may involve a technical task, written exercise or interview. Practise the format described by the employer and follow its rules on external assistance. Explain your reasoning, not just the final answer.

Prepare examples of teamwork, feedback, a mistake and initiative. Keep them consistent with the CV and cover letter. If you have no previous internship, say what your project or volunteer experience demonstrates and where you still need training.

Submit accurately and track the result

Check filenames, document format, contact details and required fields. Keep a copy of the final application and the confirmation. Monitor the official portal and email rather than relying on a social-media message.

Never pay for guaranteed selection or impersonate a referee. If someone contacts you, verify the vacancy and sender through the employer's public channels before sharing sensitive onboarding information.

Track the document, its owner and its status

A named referee is not the same as a received reference. Record every required item with its owner, format, deadline and confirmation. For project-based research placements, choose the project before tailoring the statement.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply without previous employment?+

When the vacancy permits it, use relevant coursework, projects, volunteering and student responsibilities. Explain the skill, your contribution and the output.

Should I use the same CV for every role?+

Keep one verified evidence bank, then reorder and adapt the final CV to each vacancy's responsibilities and conditions.

Does remote mean available worldwide?+

No. Confirm permitted work location, payroll or engagement arrangements, time zone and any right-to-work condition in the official vacancy.
