Find a PhD supervisor: shortlist, email and follow up

The right supervisor search starts with a research direction and the programme’s contact policy. A tailored shortlist is more useful than sending the same email to hundreds of academics.

Updated 24 Oct 2026Official source record ↓

Published by AspireCompass Editorial Team · Editorial contact: Hafiz Umar Farooq

The short answer

Find a supervisor through research fit and the programme’s official contact policy. Use a focused, personalised enquiry; supervision interest, admission and funding are separate decisions.

Next step: Check the programme route and build a shortlist with a specific research connection for each academic.

Check whether supervisor contact is part of the route

An advertised funded project may already identify a supervisor and a formal application process. A self-proposed project may require you to identify a suitable academic first. Some programmes discourage speculative contact or make allocations after admission. Read the programme instructions before emailing.

Your first decision is therefore procedural: is contact required, encouraged, optional or inappropriate? Do not use a generic email template to bypass the published route. A staff member cannot usually replace the central application or waive its mandatory requirements.

Turn a broad interest into a research direction

Write a working paragraph containing the problem, the setting or population, the method you hope to develop and why you are prepared to start. It can change during discussion; its purpose is to make your search specific enough to find relevant people.

For example, “I want to work on sustainable energy” is too broad for a shortlist. “I want to investigate reliable low-cost sensing for small energy systems, building on an undergraduate instrumentation project” gives you methods and expertise to search. Do not imply expertise in a method you have only read about.

Make a shortlist with evidence

Search department research groups, staff profiles, project advertisements and recent publications. Check whether the academic is active in your area and whether the programme can host your intended work. An old publication is a lead, not proof of current supervision availability.

Write a short first email that is easy to answer

Use the academic’s correct name and title. Explain your current stage, the concrete overlap and the question you need answered. Mention one relevant item of their work only if you understand it. A long list of publications without a connection adds little.

Attach only what the institution asks for or what is proportionate to the enquiry. A concise CV or research outline may help; a large bundle of certificates and personal identity documents usually does not. Never send bank details or passport scans in a speculative enquiry.

Teaching example

Example: a self-proposed research enquiry

Subject: Prospective PhD enquiry — low-cost sensor validation

Dear Dr [Surname], I am completing [degree] at [institution]. My recent project involved [specific task and method]. I read your group’s work on [verified topic] and was interested in [one concrete connection].

I am investigating PhD routes for [intake] and would like to ask whether you expect to consider projects in [focused direction]. I have attached a brief CV and a one-page outline, as permitted by the programme guidance. I understand admission and funding require the formal process. Thank you for considering my enquiry.

Fictional adaptable example. Replace all brackets, follow the programme contact policy and never imply you read work you have not read.

An advertised project needs a different question

If the project already gives a detailed brief and a submission link, do not ask the supervisor to repeat the advert. Identify a substantive point that affects your fit: a method, prerequisite, available direction or practical project constraint.

An enquiry should help you decide whether to apply; it should not ask for an informal admission offer.

Teaching example

Example: a question about an advertised project

Subject: Question about [project reference] before applying

Dear Professor [Surname], I am preparing an application for [verified project title]. My [project/course] included [relevant method]. The advert mentions [specific requirement]; could you clarify whether [focused, unanswered question]? I will submit through the stated portal if the project is a suitable fit. Thank you for your time.

Original teaching example. Do not send this unchanged or ask questions already answered in the advert.

Follow up with judgement, not a mass-mailing schedule

Allow reasonable time for a response, taking account of the programme deadline, leave and any stated office policy. There is no universal number of days that guarantees a good follow-up. If the institution specifies a response channel, use it.

A single concise follow-up can restate the enquiry and add relevant information. If there is no response, use the programme coordinator or another genuinely suitable contact rather than repeatedly chasing the same person. Silence does not prove your academic work is poor; availability and workload are often unknown.

Use the meeting to test fit in both directions

Prepare questions on project scope, supervision style, training, collaboration, facilities and the milestones expected in the first year. Ask what you should read or develop before applying. Be ready to explain your previous work and its limitations.

If funding is relevant, ask what the advertised support covers, whether international fees are included and how long it lasts. A friendly meeting or an expression of interest is not a formal scholarship offer, contract or admission decision.

Keep the research story consistent across documents

Your email, proposal, CV and formal application should describe the same preparation accurately. Update the research direction when a conversation changes it, but do not invent a new background for each academic.

Before submitting, check the required proposal length, ethics considerations and reference process. Retain a tracker of contacts and outcomes so that you can make a considered shortlist instead of applying to every institution that replies.

Frequently asked questions

Must I contact a supervisor before every PhD application?+

No. Advertised projects, self-proposed research and centrally allocated programmes can use different procedures. Read the programme’s contact policy first.

How many academics should I email?+

There is no useful universal target. Contact genuinely relevant people through the permitted process instead of sending bulk messages.

Does a positive reply guarantee funding?+

No. Confirm the formal admission and funding processes, conditions and written offer separately.
