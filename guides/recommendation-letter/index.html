How to get strong scholarship recommendation letters

The best referee is not always the most senior person. Choose someone who can evaluate your relevant work with specific examples and credible comparison.

Updated 6 Oct 2026

The short answer

The best referee is not always the most senior person. Choose someone who can evaluate your relevant work with specific examples and credible comparison.

Choose evidence over title

A professor who supervised your research closely can write a stronger letter than a famous academic who barely knows your work. Match each referee to the criteria they can assess.

Ask early and clearly

Request the letter four to six weeks before the deadline when possible. State the programme, deadline, submission method and why you are asking that person.

  • Current CV
  • Programme and selection criteria
  • Draft goals or essays
  • Projects completed together
  • Exact deadline and portal instructions

Help without writing the letter

A concise evidence sheet is appropriate. It reminds the referee of outcomes, dates and examples. Do not pressure a referee to sign a letter they did not write or cannot support.

Manage the submission respectfully

Send one polite reminder before the deadline, confirm receipt if the portal allows it and thank the referee. Keep them updated on the outcome.

Match referees to different criteria

When two letters are required, choose referees who add complementary evidence. One might assess academic or research readiness while another explains leadership, professional judgement or public-service impact.

Create a one-page referee brief

Provide the programme, selection criteria, deadline, submission method and a short evidence table of work completed with that referee. Mark facts that need confirmation and never write confidential praise on the referee's behalf.

Handle confidential references correctly

Use institutional or professional email addresses when requested, waive access only when you understand the form and never create an account pretending to be your referee. If a letter must be sealed or uploaded directly, respect that process.

Protect credibility and confidentiality

Do not invent a referee, write false evidence or pressure someone to sign a letter they cannot support. If the portal requires confidential submission, the referee should upload it through their own link or account.

Send a polite reminder before the deadline and confirm that the portal records completion. Keep a backup referee only where the programme permits a change.

Frequently asked questions

Can a family member write my recommendation?+

Normally no. Choose an academic or professional referee who can provide independent evidence, unless the programme explicitly permits another relationship.

What if my referee misses the deadline?+

Contact the programme through its official channel and explain briefly. Do not submit a letter yourself if the rules require direct confidential submission.

Can a friend write my recommendation letter?+

Normally no. Use an academic, research, employment or professional referee who can independently evaluate relevant work.

Should I write the letter for my referee?+

Provide an evidence sheet and programme instructions. The referee should decide the wording and submit an honest evaluation under the programme's rules.
