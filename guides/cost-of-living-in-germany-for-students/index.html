Cost of living in Germany for students: build your budget

A proof-of-funds minimum and your actual monthly spending answer different questions. Use accommodation quotes and your own university’s charges to build a budget you can use.

Updated 22 Oct 2026Official source record ↓

Published by AspireCompass Editorial Team · Editorial contact: Hafiz Umar Farooq

The short answer

Germany student living costs depend strongly on housing, city and personal circumstances. Add recurring expenses, semester charges and arrival costs; then compare them with confirmed funding without counting blocked money twice.

Start with a budget you can audit

Write down the city, course duration, household size and accommodation type. These explain more than a single national average. A room in a shared flat, a student residence and a private apartment can produce very different totals.

Use dated quotes for housing and insurance and label estimates clearly. Keep the first month separate from a normal month because deposits, travel and setup costs can distort the comparison. Review the budget when your housing or enrolment situation changes.

Recurring monthly categories

Rent is only one part of the monthly total. Check whether the quoted amount includes heating, electricity, internet and other charges. Ask about the deposit and contract duration before treating the advertised monthly figure as the complete housing cost.

Insurance costs depend on the applicable status and cover. Transport may be affected by a semester ticket or local arrangements, so check what your university contribution actually includes before adding a separate full transport pass.

Worked example

Example monthly budget: housing €520 + insurance €145 + food/household €250 + transport €35 + phone/study/personal €100 = €1,050. Twelve months cost €12,600. If semester charges total €600 and one-off arrival costs are €1,200, the first-year spending plan is €14,400. These are invented example inputs for demonstrating the method, not published fee estimates.

Tuition and semester contributions are different

Check the exact institution, degree, state and applicant category for tuition. “Public university” does not establish that every programme and applicant pays zero tuition. A semester contribution is another charge and may apply even when tuition is not charged.

Record which payments are due before enrolment and whether your award covers them. A scholarship described as living support should not be assumed to pay a tuition bill. Keep the university fee schedule attached to your budget.

Blocked account: proof of resources, not an extra expense

DAAD’s finance page gives a proof-of-funds figure explicitly dated 1 January 2025 and advises checking the competent diplomatic mission or official portal. Do not present an old figure as a confirmed requirement for a later intake.

Blocked funds are money set aside for living costs. If you budget living expenses and then add the entire deposit as a second expense, you overstate spending. Provider charges can be additional costs; the protected deposit itself is a funding arrangement. Verify the required amount and permitted evidence for your own visa situation.

Compare cities using the same applicant profile

Use the same course length, room type, insurance status and personal allowance when comparing cities. Collect two or three actual housing options per city instead of labelling one universally cheapest. A lower rent far from campus can bring higher travel costs or unsuitable contract conditions.

Before accepting an offer, test a higher-rent scenario and a delayed-funding scenario. If your plan fails in both, find a documented cost reduction or additional funding rather than assuming part-time work will immediately close the gap.

Frequently asked questions

Is the blocked-account minimum my exact monthly budget?+

No. It is a proof-of-resources requirement under the applicable rules. Your actual expenses depend on housing, insurance, university charges and circumstances.

Should I add the deposit to a full year of living costs?+

Do not count the same money twice. The deposit funds living expenses; separately include any provider fees and additional unfunded costs.

Are the example euro amounts national averages?+

No. They are explicitly illustrative inputs demonstrating the budgeting method. Replace them with current quotes.
