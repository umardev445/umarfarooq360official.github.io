Australia RTP: compare a university’s research funding offer

RTP is Australian Government research funding administered by eligible higher education providers. Start with a university’s research degree and scholarship policy, then compare the actual offer components and the money your study plan still needs.

Updated 23 Nov 2026Official source record ↓

Published by AspireCompass Editorial Team · Editorial contact: Hafiz Umar Farooq

The short answer

Apply through an eligible Australian provider for a research doctorate or research master’s. RTP can cover different components, and the university selects recipients and states the actual award. The published 2027 base and maximum stipend rates are boundaries, not your personal offer.

Next step: Find one university’s current research-degree and RTP policy pages, then record tuition, stipend, duration and uncovered costs separately.

Apply through a provider, not a national student form

The Department of Education provides RTP funding to eligible higher education providers for research doctorates and research master’s degrees. Prospective students contact the chosen provider directly; there is no RTP student application to submit to the Department. A taught master’s title is not enough to establish that the degree is an eligible research programme.

Save the exact degree, provider and intake before looking for a funding deadline. Then find the provider’s research admission and RTP scholarship instructions. They determine selection processes and timing. A central government explanation does not establish that every university has an open international award round, or that admission and scholarship assessment happen automatically through one identical process.

Separate fee offset, stipend and allowances

RTP support can include one or more components: a fees offset, a stipend and allowances for ancillary research-degree costs. Their functions differ. A tuition offset reduces the research tuition liability; a living stipend is money for general living costs. Neither label should be copied into every line of a personal budget without reading the offer.

Use a fictional comparison. Offer A includes a full tuition offset but no living stipend. Offer B includes a stated stipend but leaves its tuition treatment unresolved. A’s living gap remains; B’s tuition question must be answered before the offers are comparable. “RTP recipient” alone does not tell you which components were awarded. Ask the university to identify each component, value and period in writing.

Read the 2027 rate bounds without assuming the maximum

The government’s published full-time annual RTP stipend table gives a 2027 base of A$35,550 and a maximum of A$55,537. The 2026 figures are A$34,315 and A$53,608. These are year-specific boundaries for RTP stipends, not a promise that an applicant will receive the maximum or that every award has identical ancillary benefits.

The provider decides the actual award within the applicable rules and competitive selection process. Keep its stated annual amount, commencement date and payment arrangements with your offer. Dividing an annual figure by twelve is only a budgeting average unless the offer confirms that payment pattern. Do not call the difference between government base and maximum a guaranteed bonus available on request.

Build an original uncovered-cost calculation

Use a fictional annual living budget of A$39,600 and, solely for the exercise, a confirmed stipend equal to the published 2027 base of A$35,550. The living-only difference is A$4,050 for the year. If you also assume A$2,000 of arrival costs outside the written support, the first-year gap becomes A$6,050. Replace both cost assumptions and the stipend with your actual evidence.

A tuition offset must not be added to the cash available for rent. Keep tuition treatment on its own line and record any covered allowance without double counting it. Also distinguish a refundable deposit from spending: it can raise the money needed at arrival even though some may return later. A positive annual balance does not guarantee sufficient cash before the first payment.

Compare scholarship duration with candidature

The government describes full-time research-doctorate RTP support of three to four years at the provider’s discretion, and research-master’s support of up to two years. The university’s actual support period is necessary for planning. Do not substitute the maximum theoretical duration for the duration in your award or assume every permitted candidature extension extends the stipend.

Write a month-by-month coverage row from commencement to the intended completion date. Mark an unfunded tail if the award ends earlier, and ask how approved interruptions, leave or prior candidature affect support under the applicable policy. The student FAQ explains that time already spent in the research degree can matter to the maximum period. Your research office can assess the actual record; this guide cannot reset it to zero.

Treat international eligibility and selection as different questions

The student FAQ permits eligible domestic and international research-degree students, while universities apply competitive selection criteria and control available awards. A candidate can satisfy the general programme eligibility conditions without winning a particular university’s international stipend allocation. Ask the research office about the actual round rather than inferring funding from an admission offer.

Prepare an original evidence map linking the proposed research question, relevant preparation, supervisory environment and requested documents. Mark uncertainties honestly, including whether a supervisor endorsement, admission application or separate scholarship form is required. The government’s general RTP description does not supply universal IELTS scores or permission to skip a university requirement. Check those details with the chosen provider before paying for tests or submitting.

Check the offer before accepting or budgeting against it

Create a final offer ledger with tuition treatment, stipend amount, support period, ancillary allowances, payment prerequisites and continuation conditions. Add the university’s rules for paid work or outside activities where relevant. Do not infer an unrestricted work entitlement from the existence of a stipend; provider policies and other applicable conditions need separate checking.

In a fictional final audit, admission is confirmed but the stipend outcome is still pending. The student keeps the budget’s stipend line at zero in the confirmed-funding case and compares a separate possible-award case. That preserves the real decision: whether the plan is affordable before a funding result. Retain the university’s written offer and ask about any unclear component rather than treating a government rate table as your award letter.

Your next useful research-funding task

Choose one eligible provider and a research programme that fits your academic work. Save its current admission and scholarship pages, then fill the provider-first record and component ledger above. If the round is closed, prepare the research evidence without announcing a new national opening date. Repeat the comparison only for another plausible programme rather than collecting unrelated deadlines.

This guide uses government documents for programme boundaries and original fictional examples for planning. It has not tested an authenticated university application portal, reviewed your eligibility or confirmed a visa outcome. The useful result is a clearer set of questions and a cost calculation that can be replaced with real offer evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Do I apply to the Department of Education?+

No. Contact the chosen eligible provider; universities run the student application and selection process.

Does every RTP award pay the maximum stipend?+

No. Read the actual offer. The government table gives applicable stipend boundaries, while universities determine awards under the rules.

Can a tuition offset pay my living costs?+

It offsets the relevant tuition liability. Record a living stipend and ancillary allowances separately rather than counting fee relief as available cash.
