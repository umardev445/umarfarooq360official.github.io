The short answer

GPA is a credit-weighted average of grade points. CGPA combines the credits and grade points across the periods you enter. The target planner estimates the future average needed; it does not replace a university’s official conversion rules.

Check the grading scale before trusting a result

The same percentage can map to different grade points at different institutions. An arithmetic GPA is useful only when the inputs follow the scale your university uses. Do not treat a result on a 4-point scale as an official conversion from another system.

Worked example: credits change the result

Suppose one course has 3 credits at 4.0 and another has 2 credits at 3.0. Weighted points are 12 + 6 = 18; total credits are 5. GPA is 18 ÷ 5 = 3.60, not the unweighted average 3.50.

The default letter-grade scale may differ from your university. Select custom grade points in the calculator when you have official numeric values. Admissions may require an original CGPA statement rather than this tool's estimate.

CGPA to percentage and 4.0 GPA conversion

The converter uses a proportional estimate: current CGPA divided by the maximum scale, multiplied by 100 for percentage or by 4 for a four-point result. For example, 8.0 on a 10.0 scale produces an 80% proportional estimate and 3.20 on a 4.0 scale.

That calculation is useful for rough comparison, not credential evaluation. Institutions may examine grade distribution, course level, university policy, pass mark, class rank or a country-specific equivalency table instead of using a straight-line formula.

How to calculate the GPA needed for a target

The target planner begins with your current cumulative quality points: current GPA multiplied by completed credits. It then calculates the total quality points needed after the future credits and divides the remaining requirement by those future credits.

A required result above 4.00 is not achievable on a standard unweighted 4.0 scale with the entered credit plan. Adding more future credits can change the estimate, but course repeats, exclusions and institutional rules may produce a different official result.

  • Use the cumulative GPA currently shown by your institution
  • Enter only credits already included in that GPA
  • Add the credits you realistically expect to complete
  • Compare the required average with the maximum institutional grade point

Which GPA should go on a scholarship application?

Use the original GPA, CGPA or percentage exactly as it appears on the official transcript unless the application gives a conversion instruction. Include the scale, such as 3.42/4.00 or 8.1/10.0, so the number has context.

When a form requires a different scale, follow that programme's stated calculator or credential-evaluation route. Save a note explaining the method and do not present an AspireCompass estimate as a university-issued result.

  • Original result and maximum scale
  • Institution and country
  • Programme's required conversion method
  • Calculated result with method noted
  • Official evaluation where specifically requested

Common calculation mistakes

A simple average of course grades is wrong when courses carry different credits. Other common errors include mixing percentage marks with grade points, omitting repeated courses without permission and rounding every row before calculating the final result.

Keep several decimal places during the calculation and round only the final display. If your transcript already provides a cumulative result, do not replace it with a self-calculated number unless the application explicitly asks you to recalculate it.

Use GPA as one part of scholarship fit

A minimum GPA is normally an eligibility threshold, not a prediction of selection. Scholarships may also evaluate degree relevance, leadership, research fit, work experience, references, language evidence and the quality of programme-specific answers.

After checking the academic threshold, use the scholarship finder to narrow programmes by degree level, destination and funding. Then verify the current criteria on the programme owner's website.

Use the result in the right application route

Keep the original scale and transcript beside any estimate. A calculator helps you plan, but the university or scholarship decides how grades are evaluated and whether a converted value is accepted.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate GPA with credit hours?+

Multiply each course grade point by its credit hours, add the results and divide by the total included credit hours. The interactive calculator performs these steps automatically.

How can I calculate the GPA needed to reach a target?+

Enter your current GPA, completed credits, future credits and target GPA in the target planner. It calculates the average required across the future credits and flags a result above 4.00 as unavailable on a standard 4.0 scale.

Is CGPA divided by 10 and multiplied by 100 always correct?+

No. It is only a proportional estimate for a 10-point scale. Universities and credential evaluators may use a different institution- or country-specific method.

Can I convert percentage directly to a 4.0 GPA?+

You can create a rough proportional estimate, but an official decision may consider grade bands, pass marks, institution standards and individual courses. Follow the receiving institution's instructions.

Does a calculated GPA prove scholarship eligibility?+

No. It can help you compare an academic threshold, but official eligibility and selection depend on the complete current programme rules.

Does AspireCompass store my grades?+

No. The calculator runs in your browser and does not require an account or submission of a transcript.

Can this calculator convert my GPA into an official university GPA?+

No. It is a planning tool. Universities and credential evaluators may use their own grading rules, course weights and conversion methods, so use the institution's stated method for an official application.

Should I enter credits when calculating CGPA?+

Use credit weighting when your institution assigns different credit values to courses. A simple average can be misleading when courses carry unequal weight.

Why might my calculated result differ from my transcript?+

Your institution may apply rounding, repeated-course, excluded-course or grade-point rules that a general calculator cannot infer. Your official transcript remains authoritative.
