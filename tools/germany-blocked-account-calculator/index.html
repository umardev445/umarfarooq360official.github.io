The short answer

A blocked account is a way of demonstrating funds, not an extra cost to add twice to the same living budget. Germany’s official study-visa portal lists at least €11,904 for 2026; use the current requirement for your route and intake.

How to read the deposit and budget outputs

The blocked deposit remains your money subject to release conditions. A provider fee is a cost; a deposit is proof and a source of later living funds. The calculator supports planning and does not confirm that your consulate accepts a provider or that a future intake uses the same threshold.

What this calculator separates

The proof-of-funds calculation and the real study budget answer different questions. The first estimates money that may need to be blocked. The second estimates costs you may actually pay for housing, food, insurance, university charges and arrival.

The blocked deposit is released over time for living expenses. Adding the entire deposit on top of twelve months of living costs would count substantially the same money twice.

Use the official amount for the correct purpose

The calculator starts with the 2026 study-visa figure published by official German guidance. Applicants seeking a study place, language course or another residence purpose can face a different monthly requirement.

Keep the amount editable and confirm it through the responsible mission or Consular Services Portal immediately before transferring money. An old blog figure or another visa category is not sufficient evidence.

Scholarship reductions require confirmation

Enter only a formally confirmed monthly scholarship. The calculator can show the arithmetic difference, but the mission decides whether the award and its wording satisfy all or part of the financial-proof requirement.

For the personal budget, the same scholarship is subtracted once from total costs. Do not also reduce tuition when a tuition waiver has already been entered as zero.

Provider fees are separate from the blocked money

A provider can charge setup, monthly service or transfer-related fees. These costs are not part of the balance released for living expenses. Enter the current provider quotation rather than relying on a comparison copied from an older article.

AspireCompass does not recommend or rank blocked-account providers. Review the provider's current terms, refund route, support and payment instructions yourself.

Replace every example with dated evidence

The cost presets are starting assumptions. Replace rent with a realistic offer or housing range, tuition with the programme's fee page and semester contribution with the university's current amount.

Keep a separate cash-on-arrival total for housing deposits, travel and costs due before the first monthly release or scholarship payment.

Frequently asked questions

How is the Germany blocked account amount calculated?+

For a standard calculation, multiply the current monthly requirement by the number of required months. Any scholarship reduction must be accepted by the responsible visa authority.

Is the blocked-account deposit an extra cost?+

No. It remains the applicant's money and is released in restricted monthly amounts after arrival, although provider and transfer fees are separate costs.

Can a scholarship reduce the blocked amount?+

A recognised scholarship can be accepted as financial proof, but the responsible mission decides whether the specific award covers all or part of the requirement.

Does the calculator guarantee a student visa?+

No. It is a planning tool. The German mission assesses the official documents, current financial requirement and complete visa application.

Why can real monthly costs be higher than the official amount?+

The official amount is a standard financial threshold. Actual rent, deposits, insurance, university charges and lifestyle depend on the applicant's location and circumstances.
