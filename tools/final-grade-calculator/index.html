The short answer

Required final score = [target − current average × (1 − final weight)] ÷ final weight. This assumes the current average represents all non-final work and your course uses that weighted model.

Choose the calculation you need

The required-score mode answers what you need on the final to reach a target. The projected-grade mode answers what the overall course grade would be with an expected exam result. All scores here use a 0–100 percentage scale.

Your current average must represent the completed non-final portion after the institution’s weighting rules. If the gradebook counts future missing work as zero, or excludes categories not yet graded, first establish which average belongs in the formula.

A worked required-final example

Suppose your current average is 80%, the final counts for 40% and your target is 85%. The completed work contributes 80 × 0.60 = 48 percentage points. The final must contribute 85 − 48 = 37 points.

Keep a buffer if your institution rounds at the end or uses a separate minimum in the examination. This tool displays the arithmetic; it does not override the course’s grading policy.

Worked example

Required exam score = 37 ÷ 0.40 = 92.5%. Check: 80 × 0.60 + 92.5 × 0.40 = 85%. The final exam contributes 37 course points; that is not the same as a 37% exam score.

Understand an impossible or already secured target

A required result above 100% is outside this tool’s assumed maximum. The output should say that the target is not reachable under the entered model, rather than implying extra credit exists. If your course permits extra credit, consult its policy separately.

A result at or below zero means the arithmetic target is reached even with zero on the final. It does not establish that you may skip the examination: attendance, submission or minimum-exam conditions can still apply.

Project a result from your expected exam score

In projected-grade mode, multiply the current average by the non-final share and the expected exam score by the final share, then add the contributions. For example, 80% current work and 90% final with a 40% final weight produce 48 + 36 = 84%.

Try a cautious, expected and optimistic exam result for your own study planning. These are scenarios, not predictions. Use the gap between scenarios to decide where further preparation would matter most.

When a different calculation is required

Multiple remaining assessments, dropped scores, point-based grading and category minimums may require a fuller model. Combine completed work correctly before using this tool, and do not represent two differently weighted remaining assessments as one without calculating their combined result.

For semester or cumulative performance across courses, use the GPA calculator with credits and the correct grading scale. A course percentage is not automatically a GPA and should not be converted using an invented universal table.

Before using the output for a decision

Read the syllabus, check the remaining weight and confirm whether the displayed current average is the non-final average. Preserve the original values if you compare several targets. The reset button restores a clearly labelled example rather than an official student record.

Inputs stay in your browser for the calculation and are not submitted to AspireCompass by this tool. The result is a planning aid; only the institution issues your official grade.

Frequently asked questions

What if I need more than 100%?+

The target is not reachable under the entered 0–100 model. Check your inputs and syllabus; the calculator does not assume extra credit.

Can I skip the exam if the result is below zero?+

The numerical target may already be secured, but compulsory-exam or minimum-score rules can still apply.

Does this work with several remaining assessments?+

This version models one final component. Calculate the non-final average and remaining component correctly or use the institution’s fuller grading method.
